## 4. Phase 1: Cross-Family Model Comparison

Phase 1 answers a deployment-critical question: **among open-weight small language
models at the ~8B-parameter scale, which model family best translates natural-language
6G slice-management intents into structured actions on CPU-only infrastructure?**
Model family is the sole independent variable. Every other factor is held fixed:
the same 200-intent dataset, the same Q4_K_M quantization, the same enriched system
prompt, identical 16-core / 31 GB VMs, and a single evaluation harness scoring format
validity, action correctness, and semantic exact match. This controlled design lets us
attribute performance differences to *architecture and pre-training*, not to
confounds in size or precision.

Four families were entered: **Qwen 2.5 7B**, **Gemma 2 9B**, **Llama 3.1 8B**, and
**GLM-4 9B**. To enforce fair quantization, the Gemma 2 and GLM-4 models — whose default
Ollama tags ship as Q4_0 — were rebuilt from HuggingFace Q4_K_M GGUF files.

### 4.1 Aggregate Performance

Table 1 reports the headline metrics. Confidence intervals for exact match are 95%
normal-approximation intervals computed from the 200 per-intent scores of each model.

**Table 1 — Phase 1: Cross-Family Performance at ~8B (Q4_K_M), n = 200 intents each.**

| Rank | Family (model) | Format | Action | Exact Match (95% CI) | Latency/intent | Tok/s | Total time |
|:---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 🥇 | **Qwen 2.5 7B** | 100.0% | 43.5% | **29.8%** [25.8, 33.8] | 18.7 s | 2.2 | 62.3 min |
| 🥈 | Gemma 2 9B | 98.5% | **45.5%** | 29.3% [25.3, 33.3] | 39.8 s | 1.0 | 132.6 min |
| 🥉 | Llama 3.1 8B | 100.0% | 36.5% | 26.0% [22.2, 29.8] | 19.1 s | 2.2 | 63.7 min |
| 4 | GLM-4 9B | 100.0% | 29.0% | 24.6% [21.0, 28.2] | 22.9 s | 1.8 | 76.3 min |

Source of every cell: `results/phase1/benchmark_*.json` summaries (cross-verified against
`complete_benchmark.json`). Figure 1 visualizes the three accuracy metrics side by side.

**Four findings stand out:**

1. **Qwen 2.5 7B sits on the Pareto frontier.** It attains the highest exact match
   (29.8%), perfect format validity (100.0%), and the lowest latency of the accurate
   models (18.7 s/intent) — no other family dominates it on both accuracy *and* speed.
   Notably it is also the *smallest* model in the field (7.6B vs. 9.4B for Gemma/GLM),
   so it wins while consuming the least memory.

2. **Gemma 2 9B is a statistical tie on accuracy but a practical loser on cost.**
   Its exact match (29.3%) is within noise of Qwen (the 95% CIs overlap heavily), and
   it leads on action correctness (45.5%). But it pays **2.1× the latency** (39.8 s vs.
   18.7 s) and delivers only **1.0 tok/s** — the slowest usable decode rate in the
   study. For a real-time intent pipeline this is disqualifying.

3. **Llama 3.1 8B is the balanced runner-up.** Perfect format, competitive latency
   (19.1 s), but a 3.8-point exact-match deficit to Qwen and a markedly lower action
   score (36.5%), indicating it parses the JSON envelope well but populates fewer fields
   correctly.

4. **GLM-4 9B underperforms across every accuracy axis** despite equal size and
   quantization. Its action correctness (29.0%) is 16.5 points below Gemma. Format
   validity is perfect, so the failure is *semantic*, not structural — the model emits
   well-formed JSON with the wrong values, suggesting the enriched prompt is less well
   matched to GLM's instruction-tuning distribution.

> **Format ceiling.** Three of four families reach exactly 100.0% format
> validity, and Gemma is at 98.5%. Structural JSON compliance is therefore *not* the
> discriminator at 8B — the entire competitive signal lives in **semantic** correctness
> (which fields, which values), which is precisely what exact match and action
> correctness capture.

### 4.2 Domain-Level Decomposition

Aggregate scores hide a stark, *family-invariant* difficulty structure. Table 2
decomposes action correctness by the three intent domains.

**Table 2 — Phase 1: Action Correct by Domain (%).**

| Family | Slicing Provisioning (n=70) | Scaling Request (n=70) | Conflict Resolution (n=60) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen 2.5 7B | **100.0** | 18.6 | 6.7 |
| Gemma 2 9B | 97.1 | 25.7 | 8.3 |
| Llama 3.1 8B | 87.1 | 11.4 | 6.7 |
| GLM-4 9B | 54.3 | 21.4 | 8.3 |
| **Mean** | **84.6** | **19.3** | **7.5** |

Three difficulty tiers emerge, and the ordering
**Provisioning ≫ Scaling ≫ Conflict** holds for *every* family:

- **Slicing Provisioning — solved.** Mean 84.6% action accuracy; Qwen answers all 70
  correctly. These intents map declarative parameters (slice type, bandwidth, latency
  budget) to a template, which SLMs handle reliably.
- **Scaling Request — hard.** Mean 19.3%. Models must infer scaling *direction*
  (up/down), the target resource, and a magnitude, and they routinely confuse these
  axes. This is where Gemma's extra reasoning shows a modest edge (25.7%).
- **Conflict Resolution — near-random.** Mean 7.5%, barely above chance for a
  multi-way action choice. Resolving contention between competing slice SLAs requires
  multi-step reasoning and policy priority that ~8B SLMs simply do not possess
  zero-shot. GLM's and Gemma's small leads here are within noise.

The practical implication: **the winner is decided almost entirely by the hard
domains.** On the easy domain everyone is near-ceiling; Qwen's overall lead is built
on being least-bad where the task is genuinely difficult.

*Figure 3 — `fig3_domain_difficulty.png`: grouped bars of action correctness per domain, with tier labels.*

### 4.3 Complexity Robustness

Table 3 tracks exact match as intents move from *Simple* → *Ambiguous* → *Complex*.

**Table 3 — Phase 1: Exact Match by Intent Complexity (%).**

| Family | Simple (n=70) | Ambiguous (n=65) | Complex (n=65) | Simple→Complex drop |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen 2.5 7B | 46.4 | 20.8 | 20.9 | −25.5 |
| Gemma 2 9B | 45.3 | 21.3 | 19.9 | −25.4 |
| Llama 3.1 8B | 40.3 | 19.0 | 17.5 | −22.8 |
| GLM-4 9B | 40.5 | 15.9 | 16.2 | −24.3 |

The degradation is large (−22.8 to −25.5 points) and remarkably **consistent across
families** — every model loses roughly half its Simple-intent accuracy on Complex
intents. This tells us the complexity cliff is a property of the *task/scale regime*,
not of any one architecture. Two nuances: (i) most of the loss happens on the
Simple→Ambiguous step, implying that *lexical ambiguity* (underspecified intents), more
than multi-parameter complexity, is what breaks these models; (ii) Qwen retains its
lead at every complexity level, so its advantage is uniform rather than concentrated in
easy cases.

### 4.4 Efficiency Profile

Because these models are CPU-only and destined for edge/near-edge deployment, wall-clock
cost is a first-class metric. Latency per intent ranges **18.7 s (Qwen) → 39.8 s
(Gemma)**, a 2.1× spread. Decode throughput inversely tracks this: Qwen and
Llama sustain 2.2 tok/s, GLM 1.8, and Gemma just 1.0 tok/s. Since all models emit a
similar ~40-token JSON payload (avg tokens: 40–42), the latency differences are driven by
**per-token compute**, i.e. effective parameter count and architecture efficiency on CPU,
not by verbosity. Gemma's 9.4B width and GLM's architecture both cost throughput; Qwen's
7.6B design is the most CPU-friendly. Over the full 200-intent run this compounds to a
**2.1× wall-clock gap** (62 min vs. 133 min) between Qwen and Gemma for statistically
indistinguishable accuracy.

### 4.5 Bridge: Is the Winner Size-Limited or Family-Limited?

A natural objection to crowning Qwen is that its win might reflect *size* (7.6B) as much
as *architecture*. The Qwen size-scaling sweep in `ci_summary_v3.json` (Q4_K_M column)
lets us bound this. Exact match rises **18.5% (0.5B) → 25.8% (1.5B) → 27.3% (3B) →
29.8% (7B)**, with the 95% CIs showing the 3B→7B gain (+2.5 pt) is within noise while
the 0.5B→1.5B gain (+7.3 pt) is significant. Two consequences for Phase 1:

1. **The cross-family ranking is not an artifact of Qwen being uniquely large** — it is
   in fact the *smallest* model in the field, yet wins. Gemma and GLM are ~24% larger
   and do not beat it.
2. **Qwen already sits near its own diminishing-returns plateau at 7B**, meaning the
   43.5% action / 29.8% exact result is close to the family's ceiling under this dataset
   and quantization — a robust, not lucky, high-water mark.

This motivates Phase 2 (size scaling, kilo1) and Phase 3 (quantization, agy1), which
probe how far the winning family can be *compressed* before the Phase-1 accuracy erodes.

### 4.6 Summary of Phase 1

- **Winner: Qwen 2.5 7B** — best exact match (29.8%), perfect format, lowest usable
  latency (18.7 s), and the smallest footprint. It is the recommended default SLM for
  CPU-based 6G intent translation.
- **Accuracy is a two-horse race** (Qwen ≈ Gemma) but **efficiency breaks the tie
  decisively** in Qwen's favor (2.1× faster for equal accuracy).
- **Format is solved; semantics are not.** At 8B the differentiator is field-level
  correctness, dominated entirely by the two hard domains (Scaling, Conflict).
- **Difficulty structure is universal.** The Provisioning ≫ Scaling ≫ Conflict hierarchy
  and the ~25-point Simple→Complex cliff appear in every family, marking task properties
  rather than model quirks.
